The hero of Astro Orbit has a 3D scene, made with [three.js](https://threejs.org): a droplet in a studio, with small spheres orbiting it. This post is about what it does, and what it does on devices that can't run it.

## What it does

The droplets orbit, collide and merge. Bring the cursor toward them and they come together into one smooth form. Scroll down and they do the same.

As you scroll, a violet sphere leaves the hero and follows a track down the homepage. It turns into a card whose words light up one by one, becomes a droplet again, and turns into the first project card.

## Without a graphics chip

The scene needs a graphics chip. Without one, the browser draws 3D in software, which keeps the page busy on every frame. Before it starts, the theme checks whether the device has a graphics chip. Without one, the hero shows a still picture of the scene, and the violet sphere doesn't travel: the first project card is there straight away. three.js isn't downloaded at all on those devices.

The machines that run Lighthouse and PageSpeed Insights have no graphics chip, so they get the still picture too.

The still comes in two tones, for dark and light mode, and in three shapes: wide for a desktop, near-square for most phones, and tall for phones whose hero is taller than it's wide. Every phone also shows it for a moment, until the scene has started.

## With reduced motion

If a visitor has asked their device for less motion, the scene shows one still pose, and the sphere doesn't travel.

## Keeping it fast

The scene is the most expensive part of the site, so it only takes what the device can spare:

- It's drawn only while the hero is on screen and the tab is visible.
- With no cursor movement and no scrolling, it draws every other frame.
- When frames take too long, it lowers its quality step by step, and raises it again when there's room.

## On phones

On a phone the hero fills the screen you see when the page opens, with Safari's address bar showing. On a phone whose hero is taller than it's wide, the scene is framed by its width, so the droplet fits.
