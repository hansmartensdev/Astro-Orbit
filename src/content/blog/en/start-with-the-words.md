Most projects I join have a layout before they have a message. There's a hero, three feature cards and a testimonial strip, and the words are a grey blur of lorem ipsum waiting to be "filled in later". Later is usually the week before launch.

So I flip it: the first thing I make is a plain document with every heading, sentence and button label the page needs.

## What the words decide

Once the copy exists, most layout questions answer themselves.

- **How much space a section needs.** Three crisp benefits fit in a row. Five nuanced ones don't.
- **What the hierarchy is.** If the opening line has to work hard to explain the product, it needs room. If it doesn't, the page can get to the point sooner.
- **Which components you actually need.** A page with one clear action needs one button style, not four.

## It makes the build cheaper too

When the copy is settled before the build starts, the front-end work stops being a moving target. Strings go into translation files once, headings land at the right level the first time, and nobody is re-flowing a grid because the real headline is twice as long as the fake one.

## A small exercise

Before your next page, write it as a plain list: heading, one or two sentences, a button label. Read it aloud. If it makes sense without a single picture, the design's job gets much easier.
